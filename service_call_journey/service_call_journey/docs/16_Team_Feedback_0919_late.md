## The warranty rate card

Thirty brands, fourteen with a higher sealed-system rate. Three things about how it went in matter more than the
numbers themselves.

**It is one flat rate per claim, not a rate per task.** That is what a flat rate is, and getting it wrong would have
inflated every claim where the tech recorded more than one thing. However many lines he taps, the claim carries one
priced line and the task names describe it.

**Every brand carries the ePASS labor code your history already uses** — `WTYSZ-SZ`, `WTYGE-GE`, `WTYSEALEDSYS-KA`,
`WTY1-SPEED`. I pulled those out of the 50 warranty codes in twenty years of tickets rather than inventing a naming
scheme, so the SO3 packet is something the warranty admin can key straight in.

**The brand comes off the unit, not the payer.** A Sub-Zero billed through a home warranty company is still a
Sub-Zero, and it is Sub-Zero's card that decides.

Sealed work takes the dearer rate where you gave one: Sub-Zero $174 → **$420**, Monogram → **$350**, Fisher & Paykel
→ **$375**, the Whirlpool family → **$237.74**, GE/Café/Profile/Haier → **$225**. A brand with no sealed rate returns
the standard one *flagged* rather than quietly billing a compressor job at the door rate.

### What the history says about your card

reassuring — Sub-Zero $174 against $171.80 actual, KitchenAid $113.68 against $109.93, Liebherr $135.00 exact, Amana
$113.68 exact, LG $110 against $112.23. Two do not:

**La Cornue** — card $150, and 18 tickets in 2025–26 averaged **$702.50**.
**AGA** — card $150, 5 tickets averaged **$314**.

Both are low-volume Middleby lines, so the history may be carrying travel or a second trip the rate doesn't cover.
But at 4.7× and 2.1× I'd rather ask than quietly bill $150 on a brand where we may be leaving $550 on the table.

Whirlpool at $113.68 standard so $237.74 sealed is the obvious inference — but you named four brands for that rate
and Amana wasn't one of them, and inferring rates is how a rate card stops being trustworthy.

Speed Queen is handled as you described: $150 type A is the default and the screen says so; type B is a different
ePASS code (`WTY2-SPEED`, which your history shows at $223) and the warranty admin claims it.

## In-shop work

This one got the most thought, because everything strange about an in-shop ticket comes from one fact: **the unit is
here and nobody is waiting at home for anybody.** No window to miss, no drive time, no customer ringing at 4pm asking
where the van is. Which is exactly why they float, and exactly why they can't live at the bottom of Unscheduled
sorted by an urgency they don't have.

So they have their own lane, and it counts the thing nothing else counts: how many times a unit has been given a
bench day and still not been touched. Twice is bad luck. On the third, Mark is told by name, because there is
genuinely nobody else who's going to notice.

Placement is manual, as you asked. The part texts are muted — telling someone whose washer is on our bench that
"your part is in" is noise — but the tracker still shows them everything, because a page they choose to open isn't
an interruption.

The two ways in matter, and the tool keeps them straight. A tech who takes something off a customer's floor creates
a debt: we owe them a delivery, and the trip that takes it back has to load it. That's a line on the board card and
a line on his job screen, not a note in someone's head. A unit the customer carried in themselves ends with them
carrying it out — and their page says *ready to collect, come any time*, not *pick a window*.

The field tool has the option you asked for: **Taking it to the shop with me**, on the SO1 outcome list. It needs a
note saying what he wants to check on the bench, which is the one thing that makes the ticket useful later.

## Quotes

Every quote reviewed, no threshold. Three days of silence rather than fourteen.

And the one that changes how the office thinks about a dead quote: **a closed quote keeps its lines and can be
reopened.** Declined, shopping, no-response or diagnostic-only, all reopenable. The old closure is kept rather than
overwritten, the ticket goes back to SO2.1, and the button you asked for puts the $169.95 they already paid on as a
credit — ticked by default, still a choice. Someone who thought about it for a week and said yes isn't charged twice
for the same visit, and Noell isn't rebuilding a quote she already built.

The same logic finished the discontinued-part argument. Charge and credit are no longer two options to weigh up: the
default is charge, and charging now *means* the customer is told we credit it in full against a replacement from us.
Waive is still one click.

## Confirming a route

Half-day windows stay. What changed is when the customer hears. Nobody is texted until the dispatcher presses
**Confirm route** — and then everyone on that day is, with the date, the window and the tech's name.

Every message is shown first, all of them on one screen, so a wrong name is caught by the person pressing the button
rather than by the customer. The day gets pinned. A stop moved afterwards is an ordinary reschedule with its own
text. Booking horizon is two calendar weeks now rather than ten business days — nearly the same thing until a
holiday week, when it isn't.

## The ZIPs — and something worth reading

Your five went in as you gave them: 78741 and 78652 to Josh, 78726 to Chris, 78611 to Trevor, 78209 office-booked
with the 281-corridor exception. The rest went to whoever covers the nearest area.

But **six of the 47 unmapped ZIPs are not places.** 75006 is MILESTONE — a builder, 17 calls. 91403 is NEW ORLEANS
TRUST, 9 calls. 98103 is DOWBUILT. 77429 is LG warranty. The appliance was never in Dallas or Seattle; that's the
*billing* address on the account, and it has been sitting in the ZIP field where a routing decision reads it.

It's the payer-versus-customer problem from the history load, showing up in a place nobody was looking. Those six are
marked as billing addresses and will never be zoned. The question underneath it — open item 53 — is whether the
intake form is reliably capturing a service address separately from the account address, because if it isn't, this
keeps happening and we just won't see it.

## One link per household

Done, keyed on the **address** rather than the account, the same rule as everywhere else — two accounts at one house
share the link, a namesake across town doesn't. A house with two repairs open shows both on the one page.

The landlord case you're content to defer is written into the spec as open item 54 so it isn't rediscovered: a tenant
holding the household link would see the landlord's other calls at that address. The fix when it bites already exists
in the data model — the `visit_only` flag on a job contact.
